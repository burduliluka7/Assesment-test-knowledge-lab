# Verified primary references and borrowing boundaries

Checked against primary publisher or author-hosted sources during this implementation. These four papers position one small method; they are not four additional algorithms to implement.

1. **Schaub, Michael T.; Li, Jiaze; Peel, Leto (2023). “Hierarchical community structure in networks.” Physical Review E 107, 054305.** [Publisher and DOI](https://journals.aps.org/pre/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevE.107.054305). Borrowed: the need to distinguish a hierarchy produced by an algorithm from identifiable hierarchical community structure. Difference: this paper studies stochastic externally equitable partitions and hierarchical inference; TSHC fixes display budgets and makes no generative identifiability claim. Temporal native hyperedges require explicit visibility and endpoint-group tracking absent from a static pairwise display hierarchy.

2. **Chen, Jie; Saad, Yousef; Zhang, Zechen (2022). “Graph coarsening: from scientific computing to machine learning.” SeMA Journal 79, 187–223.** [Publisher and DOI](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40324-021-00282-x); [author PDF](https://www-users.cse.umn.edu/~saad/PDF/ys-2022-01.pdf). Borrowed: recursive aggregation and the importance of stating what a coarse representation preserves. Difference: TSHC retains native hyperedge identity and multiplicity rather than claiming spectral approximation guarantees for a reduced graph. Evolving input also requires historical membership regularization and persistent identity bookkeeping.

3. **Zhou, Dengyong; Huang, Jiayuan; Schölkopf, Bernhard. “Learning with Hypergraphs: Clustering, Classification, and Embedding.” Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 19, 1601–1608 (NIPS 2006 conference; proceedings published 2007).** [Original proceedings PDF](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2006/file/dff8e9c2ac33381546d96deea9922999-Paper.pdf). Borrowed: treating multi-object relationships as hyperedges rather than immediately discarding their grouping. Difference: their normalized spectral framework is not our normalized entropy fragmentation objective. We do not claim an irreducibly higher-order advantage over every pairwise statistic; our guarantee concerns the serialized edge identity, arity and multiplicity. Temporal evidence restrictions are an additional requirement here.

4. **Chi, Yun; Song, Xiaodan; Zhou, Dengyong; Hino, Koji; Tseng, Belle L. (2007). “Evolutionary Spectral Clustering by Incorporating Temporal Smoothness.” Proceedings of the 13th ACM SIGKDD International Conference on Knowledge Discovery and Data Mining (KDD ’07).** [Author-hosted original paper](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/evospe.pdf). Borrowed: an explicit tradeoff between fitting current observations and retaining historical structure during optimization. Difference: TSHC uses shared-node VI toward the previous partition at each hierarchy level, with restricted greedy merges, rather than an evolutionary spectral relaxation. Newly visible nodes have no historical penalty. Hungarian matching happens afterward and is not regularization.
