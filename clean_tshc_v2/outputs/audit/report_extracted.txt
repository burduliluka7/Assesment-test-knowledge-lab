                                                Abstract
         This report documents a frozen, development-only redesign of an entity retrieval pipeline
     over a materials-informatics knowledge graph snapshot. The prior V1 system and a rst V2
     attempt both failed at the candidate-admission stage: the correct answer entities were usually
     absent from the top of the candidate list, so no reranking strategy could repair the outcome.
     The redesign therefore targets admission rst: canonical entity grouping with graph-provided
     aliases and type families, a separately indexed evidence store derived from inferred schema
     roles, six retrieval lists (dense over the raw question, dense over a structured view, BM25,
     safe name matching, evidence-dense, and bounded graph expansion  ve channel families if
     the two dense views are counted as one), a deterministic union, and reciprocal rank fusion. A
     genuine MS MARCO relevance cross-encoder reranks a xed prex, and a pinned local natural-
     language-inference model is used as an explicitly uncalibrated requirement-verication proxy
     with four-status semantics over a stable top-20 tiering. Every entity channel applies the same
     hard answer-family type lter; there is no soft type prior in this pipeline. The predeclared
     hypothesis is partly supported. Candidate recall rose decisively: the A5 fusion arm admits
     37/49 directly mapped target occurrences by depth 100, 44/49 by depth 500, and 46/49 in the
     full union, against 3/49 at depth 100 for the previous V2 high-recall arms. Final ranking did
     not follow proportionally. The declared primary system A7 recovers 10 of 63 target occurrences
     at top-10 (15.87 %), against 3/63 for the previous V2's dense baseline and 2/63 for its relevance
     arm, but a predeclared smaller-pool control, A6_100, reaches 12/63; mean reciprocal rank is
     higher for the simple round-robin union A4 (0.2013) than for A5 (0.1554), A6 (0.1480) or
     A7 (0.1516). Among the 44 target occurrences admitted to the 500-deep reranking pool, 18
     moved up and 26 moved down, with the conditional median rank moving from 39 to 50.5.
     High recall did not deliver proportional ranking quality, and the larger reranking pools were
     worse on top-10 recovery here. All numbers are development diagnostics from frozen artifacts
     of a single seeded run; no held-out, variance or inferential-superiority claim is made.

1 Motivation
1.1 What V1 and the rst V2 established
The task is a small set of expert-authored scientic questions of the form Which methods (by Feb
2026) are best suited for . . .  and What are the leading . . . , each annotated with one or more
target answer entities. The retrieval corpus is a knowledge graph snapshot containing typed nodes
(method, technique, component, cited_work, dataset, metric, article) and typed relations.

   V1 used a single dense bi-encoder over a bounded textual rendering of each graph node. Its fail-
ure mode was not subtle ranking error but outright absence: in the current matched re-run, the V1-
like control A0 admits only 6 of 49 directly mapped target occurrences anywhere in a 500-candidate
list, and recovers 0 at top-10. The rst V2 iteration, archived under archive/flat_20261001,
attempted richer scoring without xing admission; its high-recall arms (V2-B, V2-C) admitted
only 3 of 49 mapped occurrences at depth 100 and recovered 1 and 2 occurrences respectively at
top-10, with MRR 0.0168 and 0.0566, while its own dense baseline arm recovered 3.

   Two lessons drive this redesign.    First,   admission constrains everything downstream : each
later component is conditioned on a pool it cannot extend, so recall at realistic depths must be
                                                       rank statistics must be explicit about
measured and reported before any reranking claim. Second,
misses. The archived arms' rank means were computed over the full corpus, where every target
necessarily has some rank and the miss count is zero by construction. Those gures are valid under
that protocol; they are simply a dierent quantity from the conditional means over a truncated
candidate list reported here, and the two are not comparable without also reporting misses.



1.2 Development-only status
The benchmark questions and their gold annotations were already inspected by the author during
V1 development, and that exposure motivated this redesign. Separating prediction from evaluation
at execution time (Section 11) removes    execution-time leakage: the predictor cannot read gold
les, historical outputs, reports, notes, or the evaluator. It does not and cannot remove the fact
that the benchmark had already inuenced the design and the request. No claim is made here
about the precise extent of any design-time exposure beyond that. Consequently:


    every number in this report is a development diagnostic;
    no claim of inferential or statistical superiority over V1, prior V2, or any external system is
     made or justied;

    with 14 scored questions and 63 target occurrences, dierences of one or two occurrences
     are at the resolution of the measurement and are reported as observations, not as eects.



1.3 Predeclared hypothesis
Hypothesis. Replacing single-channel dense node retrieval with entity-centric canonical group-
ing, a separately indexed evidence store, multi-channel deterministic union and rank fusion will
(H1) raise candidate recall of directly mapped target occurrences at operationally useful depths
by a large margin, and (H2) that this improved admission, combined with a genuine relevance
cross-encoder and a requirement-verication reranking stage, will translate into materially better
top-10 identity recovery.

   Verdict: partly supported. H1 is supported by the observed development evidence on
candidate recall (Section 12.1); the measurement is a single run on a design-exposed question set,



                                                  3
so the support is for the recall claim as measured, not an unqualied general result. H2 is mixed.
Top-10 recovery rose from 3/63 (previous V2 dense baseline) and 2/63 (previous V2 relevance
arm) to 10/63 for A7, but that overall comparison involves a redesigned index, new channels
and new fusion, and therefore does not isolate the contribution of the cross-encoder. Within the
matched new run the incremental reranking picture is mixed: top-10 hits move 6 (A5) → 9 (A6)
→ 10 (A7) while MRR moves 0.1554 → 0.1480 → 0.1516, and a predeclared smaller-pool control
beats the declared primary on top-10.

3 Canonical entities, aliases and type families
Surface normalization applies, in order: Unicode NFKC, casefolding, whitespace collapse, and
typographic-hyphen normalization to ASCII hyphen. All other punctuation is             preserved. Writing
ν(·) for this normalizer, two member nodes mi , mj merge into one canonical entity only if

                  ν(name(mi )) = ν(name(mj ))    ∧        F(type(mi )) = F(type(mj )),                 (1)


   where F maps a node type to its family. The approach family is {method, technique, component, cited_work}
dataset, metric and article each form their own singleton family. Equation (1) requires iden-
tical normalized strings: no fuzzy, embedding-based or abbreviation-based merging is performed,
because an inferred merge would assert an identity the graph does not.

   The canonical identier of an entity is derived from the        lowest member node ID, never a
gold label and never a human-chosen preferred name, which keeps the identier space independent
of the annotation used later for evaluation.

   Aliases are taken from the graph only. Actual graph aliases are used as             retrieval strings 
they enter the name channel and the BM25 lexical document  but they never induce a merge
under Equation (1). An alias relation in the graph is evidence that a string refers to an entity;
string similarity between two entity names is not.



3.1 Hard answer-family ltering
Answer type families map a question's extracted answer head(s) to admissible entity types:
method, model, architecture, algorithm, tool and system map to the approach family;
technique maps to (technique, method, component); benchmark maps to (dataset, metric);
dataset and metric map to themselves; and article/paper map to (article, cited_work).
   This ltering is   hard, and it is applied identically by every entity channel. A single
eligible set is computed once per question as

                                                     S
                        A(q) =       E : types(E) ∩       t∈heads(q) Fam(t) ̸= ∅   ,                   (2)


   with the convention that an empty or unmapped head set admits everything. The two dense
channels, BM25, the name channel, the evidence channel and the graph expansion all restrict their
output to A(q).   There is no soft type prior anywhere in this pipeline. The conguration le
retains a legacy key soft_type_prior = 0.8; it is     unused by this implementation and is reported
here only so that a reader inspecting config.json is not misled. Consequently TypedRaw is not
A1 plus hard ltering: both arms are hard ltered, and TypedRaw isolates the         raw-question dense
view alone under that lter, while A1 takes the maximum over the raw and structured dense views
under the same lter. The only unltered arm is A0, which is the V1-like dense control over graph
nodes.

   The hard lter has a measured cost: three EXACT target occurrences in Q14 (JARVIS-DFT,
AFLOW, Alexandria) are excluded (Section 12.6). Q14 asks for datasets and benchmarks, whose
families are {dataset, metric}; the graph nodes bearing those three names carry node types
outside that set, so Equation (2) removes them before any channel runs. The honest description
is therefore that these are scientic targets the question requests      as datasets/benchmarks whose
graph typing diers from the requested families; no claim is made here about what the graph does
type them as, because that was not inspected for this report.

4 Query representation
The   original question is retained verbatim, including every modier, and it is the string used
as the relevance query for the cross-encoder. Extractive structure is strictly supplementary. A
deterministic extractive parser produces:


    the answer head(s) and the corresponding type families;

    preserved pre-head scientic modiers  e.g. universal in universal MLIPs, twisted van
       der Waals in Q3  kept attached rather than stripped as stopword-like qualiers, since in
       this domain the modier usually carries the discriminating content;


    coordinated answer families, so that datasets and benchmarks in Q14 yields a union of
       admissible families rather than an arbitrary single choice;


    core requirements, additional requirements and exclusions, used only by the verication
       stage.


     Two query views are scored independently: the original question qo and a structured rendering
qs . A third, parsed-only view qp is encoded solely to drive the ParsedOnly control and never enters
the union. ParsedOnly is markedly worse than the original-question arms (MRR 0.0843 versus
0.1666 for A1). The supportable reading is narrow: on these 14 questions, with this encoder and
this rendering, replacing the raw question by the structured rendering lost ranking quality, which is
why the raw question is retained. It does not establish that extractive parsing destroys information
in general, and indeed the structured dense view recovers slightly   more mapped occurrences than
the original-question view (21 versus 19, Table 5).




5 Indexed evidence and role inference
5.1 Separate evidence encoding
Every evidence node is encoded    separately by type: claim, task, problem, technique, component,
dataset, metric, article.        Each entity links to its evidence with source IDs, relation paths,
provenance and candidate-specic ags. Encoding per node rather than concatenating an entity's
evidence into one document is a deliberate design choice: a question typically matches one specic
claim or task, and concatenation would average that signal into the rest of the entity's text.

   Methodobject roles are inferred from    unique member type signatures, never from member
order in the underlying tuple. The supported patterns are:


  1. one method plus typed task/problem/technique/ component/dataset/metric members;


  2. one article plus one claim, optionally plus one method;


  3. one article presenting one method;


  4. one article citing cited_work targets.


   Anything ambiguous  in particular        extends relations with an unresolvable direction, or
relations whose member types do not match a supported signature  is omitted and recorded
as a rejected edge.   This is   schema inference, not annotated linguistic role labelling and not
entailment. A role arc states that the graph's typed structure is consistent with a reading, not
that the reading is semantically veried.




                                                  6
5.2 Safe mention attachment
Claims are attached to entities by exact, bounded mentions of entity names or graph aliases. Short
generic words are excluded: a string is admissible as a mention anchor only if it is at least three
characters and either contains digits, is CamelCase, is all-caps of length ≥ 3, or is a multiword
name of at least ten characters. This blocks the pathological case in which a generic token used in
ordinary prose attaches unrelated claims. Article titles supply provenance context for an entity;
they do   not license attaching all claims from the same article to that entity.
   The evidence index itself is    uncapped  its purpose is recall. Per-family caps apply only
when selecting text for the cross-encoder card (Section 8).




6 Bounded graph expansion
Graph generation admits exactly two path shapes:




               evidence −→ method                                                    (1 hop),            (3)

               evidence −→ article −→ presented/cited entity                         (2 hops).           (4)


   Only     article nodes may be intermediate.             There is no author traversal, no unrestricted
spreading activation, no recursion and no hierarchy beam. Parameters are xed: top-200 evidence
seeds, article degree cap 80, fanout cap 64, maximum 2 hops, path decay γ = 0.8. A path p of
edge length ℓ(p) from seed sp with seed score σ(sp ) contributes



                       g(E) =      max       γ ℓ(p)−1 σ(sp ),       γ = 0.8, ℓ(p) ≤ 2,                   (5)
                                 p: sp −→E

   and only destinations in A(q) are kept, so the hard type lter applies to graph expansion as
well. Each surviving entity exports up to eight scored path traces.

   Two limits must be stated explicitly. First,      direction inference is limited: where the graph
does not unambiguously assert which of two entities extends or specializes the other, the arc is
dropped rather than guessed, so some legitimate paths are simply unavailable. Second,              citation
paths are not entailment. That article A cites work W places W in A's structural context;
it says nothing about whether W satises the question's requirements.                 Accordingly, citation-
derived evidence is admitted as structural context for           retrieval but is categorically ineligible as
a premise for requirement verication (Section 9).

11 Anti-leakage order and evaluation protocol
The execution order is xed and one-directional:


   1. All predictions are produced and written to disk.


   2. Prediction artifacts are frozen under SHA256. The frozen prediction digest is
      5bc46c27e260572cc33630ff59b3dd142cc37ca5ef66d13eb77f29b1a2ad0eda.
   3. A   separate entry point veries every frozen digest, and only then imports the evaluator and
      reads gold and target annotations.


   During step 1, prediction audit hooks deny reads of gold lenames, historical outputs, reports
and notes, protected project source, and any evaluator import; permitted third-party packages
are read-only. These hooks are a procedural discipline for ordinary Python execution; they are
explicitly   not hostile native-code containment. All local models carry xed revisions and
per-le hashes (Table 16), and model downloads require an explicit download ag. The run uses
the pre-existing interpreter's third-party installation with -B and no runtime project imports from
rest_of_work.

12.7 Missing evidence versus contradiction
Of 800 requirement components, only 376 had an eligible premise;          the remaining 424 are
NOT_SUPPORTED_BY_AVAILABLE_EVIDENCE purely because the candidate-specic index,               with
article-level and structural-path evidence excluded, contained nothing to score. Adding the 270
neutral NLI outcomes gives the reported total of 694.

   The status counts aggregate positive and exclusion components, whose polarities are opposite
under Equation (11).      The 71 SUPPORTED statuses therefore comprise positive components with
P (entail) ≥ 0.7 and P (contra) < 0.3 together with exclusion components with P (contra) ≥ 0.7
and P (entail) < 0.3; symmetrically for the 35 CONTRADICTED statuses. Reading the aggregate as
a single entailment-probability condition would be wrong unless exclusion components happen to
be absent, which the hando does not report.

13 Limitations
  1.   Development-only, design-exposed.            The questions and gold annotations were seen
       during V1 development, and that exposure motivated this work. Execution-time separation
       does not make this a held-out result.      Nothing here supports a publication claim or an
       inferential comparison.


  2.   Scale. 14 scored questions, 63 target occurrences, 49 directly mappable. One occurrence is
       1.6 percentage points of R@k . The A7-versus-A6_100 top-10 gap is two occurrences.


  3.   Annotation ceiling. 14 of 63 occurrences (22 %) cannot receive identity credit under any
       system, and two of 14 questions have no creditable target at all. Maximum attainable R@k
       is 49/63 = 77.8 %; maximum attainable MRR is 12/14 = 0.857.


  4.   Index sparsity (schema coverage). 2508 of 2978 entities have no candidate-specic
       evidence.   The evidence channel, the graph channel's evidence seeding, the cross-encoder
       card and the verication stage are all structurally weaker for those entities.      This is a
       coverage property of the graph. Those entities remain reachable by the dense channels and
       by BM25 over names and aliases.


  5.   Schema inference is structural. Role arcs come from unique typed member signatures;
       ambiguous extends arcs and signature mismatches are dropped rather than resolved, so
       some legitimate paths are unavailable, and citation paths provide structural context and are
       never treated as entailment.


  6.   Year-granular time ltering. By Feb 2026 is enforced at year granularity; month
       precision is unrecoverable from year-only evidence.   first_seen_year records publication
       visibility in this graph, not an actual discovery date, and the undated-alias suppression rule
       only applies at cutos earlier than a node's last observation.


  7.   No calibration and no tuning. Cross-encoder logits are not probabilities; NLI proba-
       bilities are uncalibrated. Path decay (0.8), RRF k (60), evidence/graph depths (500/200),
       family caps, the 512-token budget and the NLI threshold (0.7) are xed design constants; no
       hyperparameter search was performed, so none is claimed optimal. The soft_type_prior
       key in config.json is legacy and unused.




                                                   21
   8.   Controls beyond their prex. For A6_100 and A6_200, nal ranks beyond the pool size
        come from the unchanged RRF tail, not from reranked entities, and their candidate gures
        in Table 4 are capped at the pool. Comparing R@100 across pool sizes compares dierent
        objects below the prex boundary.


   9.   Single run, no variance. Every number is one seeded execution. No variance estimate and
        no hypothesis test exists, so dierences of one or two occurrences are reported as observations
        only.


 10.    Ranks are not scientic adjudication. The metrics score agreement with a small an-
        notation set.    A high-ranked non-gold entity is not shown to be a wrong answer, and a

14 Future work
Two questions are open, and neither can be settled by rerunning arms that already exist in this
study.   A6_100 is such an arm: it is reported above as a predeclared control, so a cascade that
reranks only the top 100 is a conguration already measured here, not a new experiment, and
quoting its 12/63 as a prediction would be reporting a known result.


   1.   Pool choice needs independent data. The pool-size pattern (top-10 hits 12/9/9; condi-
        tional median 27/38/50.5) comes from the same 14 design-exposed questions used to build
        the system. The appropriate next step is to assemble an        independent question and target
        set  new questions, annotated without reference to this system's outputs  and to regis-
        ter the pool-size comparison on that set before running it. Until then the pool choice is a
        development observation.


   2.   Evidence packing is a hypothesis, not a nding. The large downward movements
        (Q12 MACE 42 → 302, Q12 PET 61 → 317) have exported card contents: included blocks,
        omitted blocks and token counts are already in the frozen artifacts. The honest sequence is
        to   inspect those existing traces rst, descriptively, and only then preregister a comparison of
        alternative evidence-packing policies  dierent family caps, dierent block order, best-t
        instead of prex packing  on new data.           No such comparison is predeclared here, and
        no tuning of packing on the present benchmark should be presented as a held-out result
        afterwards.


   Nothing in the present data justies extending the verication stage; its measured gain is
one occurrence and should be held xed while the questions above are addressed.               The three

15.1 Two distinct determinism checks
These are dierent checks with dierent scopes and are not interchangeable:


  1.   Replay check. The replay reuses cached model outputs and veries that the full prediction
       byte stream reproduces identically against the frozen digest
       5bc46c27e260572cc33630ff59b3dd142cc37ca5ef66d13eb77f29b1a2ad0eda.
       This establishes byte-identical replay of the deterministic pipeline logic  grouping, ltering,
       union construction, RRF ordering, tie-breaking, card packing, tier assignment, serialization
          given the cached model outputs. It does not re-execute the neural models and therefore
       says nothing about reproducibility of an uncached full-model run.


  2.   Fixed-input model determinism tests. Separate synthetic tests feed xed inputs to the
       pinned    dense encoder and the pinned relevance cross-encoder and check that the inference
       output is bit-stable, with deterministic algorithms enabled, when those models are available.
       The   NLI verication model is not covered by this check; the NLI model is covered only
       by the status-semantics and policy tests, which assert that it is never used as a relevance
       scorer.


   Together they establish deterministic pipeline logic given cached outputs, and bit-stable infer-
ence for two of the three models given identical inputs. Neither establishes  nor is claimed to
establish  stability across hardware, library versions, uncached reruns, or re-trained checkpoints.
