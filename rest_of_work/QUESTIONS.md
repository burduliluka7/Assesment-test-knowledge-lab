# Interpretations requiring assessor clarification

These questions concern genuine ambiguities, not facts discoverable by inspecting the files. Work continues under the conservative assumptions below; the assistants have not contacted assessors.

1. **Fact date versus evidence availability.** Metadata defines edge.year as a fact date or paper date, while provenance.article_year identifies the asserting paper. We require both dates and every member's first_seen_year to be at or before the cutoff. We defer whole edges, never truncate endpoints. This yields fewer early edges than an edge.year-only slice.
2. **February 2026 benchmark versus annual dates.** Questions specify February 2026, but article/node records have only years. Primary retrieval uses a separate 2025 hierarchy conditioned on the latest earlier main snapshot; the 2026 sensitivity may include later evidence. The main hierarchy sequence remains 2020→2022→2024→2026.
3. **Expected method granularity and alias mapping.** Ground truth names methods/families without node IDs, and `expected_methods` also includes datasets and benchmarks. The primary retrieval view is explicitly method-node-only. Exact normalized matches take precedence; otherwise whole-token matches produce an explicitly flagged candidate set. Hyphens and plus signs are retained, so DeepH is not silently equated to DeepH-E3, nor MACE to MACE-MP-0. These are heuristic alignments, not assessor-adjudicated ground truth. We report mapping coverage and mapped recall plus an all-target lower bound; missing types/aliases reduce coverage.
4. **Claim-only questions and claim alignments.** Four questions contain required_claims rather than expected_methods. They are not applicable to method retrieval. Supporting claim paraphrases lack verified node alignment, so supporting-claim recall is unavailable. No embedding similarity is substituted for truth.
5. **Unversioned surface forms.** The export contains one current surface form per entity, even for entities seen in earlier papers. We filter nodes/evidence by first appearance, but cannot verify whether that wording includes later updates. Strict historical text honesty requires versioned extraction records or original paper text.

Resolved by inspection: 18 questions are supplied rather than the brief's rough count of 17; historical origins reach 1901, while first corpus appearances begin in 1997. Intermediate budgets and objective weights are expressly configurable design choices, not questions needing assessor permission.
